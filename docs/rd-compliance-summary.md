# R&D Compliance Summary

**Status:** Public architecture control — not a tax return, legal opinion, or statement of an approved credit amount.

This document explains the evidence discipline used to evaluate potential research activities under Internal Revenue Code Section 41. It contains patterns and controls only. Private tax records, payroll, bank records, contracts, participant data, and individual allocations remain in restricted systems.

## Core rule

A research-credit position must be supported by the taxpayer, a defined business component, the four-part test, an allowable expense category, and a traceable connection between the technical activity and the cost.

Genealogical history, community mission, cultural impact, reparative-justice objectives, grant narratives, and social need may explain why a system was designed. They do not independently create or substantiate an R&D tax credit.

## Business-component discipline

Potential components are evaluated separately. Current working components include:

- CCOS™
- Freedom Notes / participant rewards and accounting ledger
- LedgerGuard
- Nexus Stability AI
- automated workflow concepts
- VR and immersive technical modules

A project name alone is not enough. Each component requires a canonical owner, taxpayer, technical scope, uncertainty statement, experiment record, result, and expense nexus.

## Four-part test evidence

For each component, the restricted evidence system should identify:

1. **Permitted purpose** — the intended new or improved function, performance, reliability, or quality.
2. **Technical uncertainty** — what capability, method, or appropriate design was unknown at the beginning.
3. **Process of experimentation** — alternatives considered, prototypes, tests, failures, revisions, and measured conclusions.
4. **Technological basis** — the computer-science, engineering, physical-science, or biological-science principles relied upon.

## Required evidence packet

Each candidate activity should be linked to:

- a dated architecture decision record, issue, commit, test log, prototype, or deployment record;
- the person who performed the work and that person's employment or contractor classification;
- time records or contractor deliverables;
- payroll, invoice, payment, and general-ledger support;
- U.S.-research confirmation;
- retained-rights and financial-risk analysis for contract research;
- a funding-source and double-counting review; and
- CPA disposition.

## Exclusion and separation controls

The following activities are not treated as qualified merely because they support a technology project:

- routine administration and management;
- fundraising and investor relations;
- marketing, branding, and public relations;
- curriculum writing and workforce instruction;
- clinical or peer-support service delivery;
- film, documentary, music, story, and artistic-content production;
- legal, securities, tax, or regulatory analysis by itself;
- tokenomics and economic-model design by itself;
- surveys, market studies, and ordinary user feedback;
- planned integrations that were not actually configured and used contemporaneously.

Costs are also screened against film incentives, WOTC, grants, reimbursements, clinical funding, and other tax benefits to prevent double counting.

## Labor-allocation control

A percentage allocation is an internal hypothesis, not an automatic qualification rule. Labor is accepted only when contemporaneous records identify the date, business component, technical task, uncertainty, experiment, result, and corroborating artifact.

Unpaid or in-kind founder time is not treated as a wage expense.

## Public-language controls

Public materials use conservative descriptions:

- **Freedom Notes:** “closed-loop participant rewards and accounting ledger under legal review.”
- **CCOS:** “designed for HIPAA-aligned data separation and consent controls.”

Public materials do not declare securities-law, money-transmission, banking, HIPAA, clinical, or tax compliance without the applicable professional review and supporting evidence.

## System-of-record separation

- **GitHub public repository:** architecture patterns, governance summaries, and public controls.
- **Restricted source repositories:** code, tests, failures, architecture decisions, and technical evidence.
- **Google Drive / document system:** contracts, narratives, version histories, and CPA-facing workpapers.
- **Airtable control register:** business-component status, evidence gates, candidate costs, and CPA disposition.
- **Accounting system:** payroll, invoices, payments, funding sources, capitalization, and general-ledger records.
- **Genealogy system:** lineage evidence and historical-source controls, maintained separately from tax substantiation.

## No approved amount stated here

This public repository does not state or validate any QRE total, research-credit amount, payroll-tax offset, amended-return position, or filing deadline compliance. Those determinations belong to the taxpayer's CPA and tax counsel after source reconciliation.
